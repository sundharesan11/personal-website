# Voice & character

The site is one person talking. Every word of copy is in this register. Don't drift into corporate/portfolio-speak.

## The character
Sundharesan — an engineer who writes and draws. Confident, quick, a little sardonic, genuinely warm. Knows he's good, refuses to be pompous about it. A storyteller by instinct: he frames builds, ideas, books, and life as stories (a problem, some tension, a third act).

## How he sounds
- **First person, conversational** — like he's explaining his work to you across a table (or at a bar), not presenting slides.
- **Dry wit + light self-aware sarcasm.** Funny where it *earns* it — never joke-stuffed, never mean. One good dry line beats three okay ones.
- **Confidence shown through specifics, undercut with humour.** State the real thing, then deflate it a touch. ("…prove it isn't just vibes." / "wait, it actually scales.")
- **Crisp and editorial.** Short. Magazine standfirsts and leads, not essays. Cut every word that's just delivering info.
- **Concrete > generic.** Real images, real stakes. No buzzword soup, no "passionate about leveraging synergies."
- **Storyteller framing** runs through everything — the through-line between his engineering and his writing.

## Mechanics
- Em-dashes for rhythm; the occasional parenthetical aside *(like this)*; sparing italics for emphasis.
- Allowed to break a "rule" for effect. Sentence fragments are fine. A wink is fine.
- Lowercase isn't required, but keep it relaxed — not shouty.

## Litmus test
If a line reads like it could appear on any competent engineer's LinkedIn, it's wrong. Rewrite until it could only be him.
