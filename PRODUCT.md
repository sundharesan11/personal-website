# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People who land on a personal site and decide, in about thirty seconds, whether this person is worth their time. Two audiences matter most:

- **Founders and collaborators** weighing whether to build with Sundharesan. They care about range, taste, and how he thinks, not a CV.
- **Curious readers** with no specific ask, who should want to stay and actually read rather than skim and bounce.

The job to be done: understand who he is fast, then explore. The site is the threshold to his work, writing, reading, and ambitions.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Sundharesan Kumaresan, structured as an editorial publication (a magazine, not a CV). It presents one coherent person, an AI engineer who also writes fiction, reads widely, and is forming ventures, under a single through-line: fairness and legibility, who a system sees and who it leaves out. Success is a stranger leaving with the sense of a real, layered person and the itch to open one more section.

## Brand Personality

First-person, casual, dry-witted, warm. A storyteller by instinct who frames work, ideas, and life as stories with tension and a third act. Confident about being good without being pompous, quick to undercut himself with humour. Honest to a fault: the site states only what is true (modelling is a desire, not a fact; the ventures are intentions kept on the To page, not active work), and that candor is the brand. Three words: casual, sardonic, warm.

## Anti-references

This must NOT look or read like:

- A **generic dev-portfolio template**: grid of project cards, skills bars, "passionate developer" copy, tech-logo soup.
- A **corporate / SaaS landing page**: hero-metric blocks, gradient CTAs, feature grids, marketing-speak, "trusted by" logos.
- A **flashy, over-animated showcase**: scroll-jacking, gratuitous motion, effects that perform instead of serve.
- An **art / design folio**: anything implying he is a designer, illustrator, or visual artist. He is not. Imagery is photographic or typographic, never decorative craft-signalling (no doodles-as-identity, no borrowed film stills as a mood board).

## Design Principles

1. **Honesty is the brand.** Never overclaim. Aspirations are labelled as aspirations; an empty section is shown empty before it is ever faked. Candor earns trust the visuals cannot.
2. **One person, several registers.** Code, fiction, reading, and ambition are one story told different ways, not separate selves. Every section should ladder back to the fairness-and-legibility thread.
3. **Editorial, not template.** Compose like a magazine: flush-left asymmetry, large Didone display type, full-bleed photography only where it earns its place, generous whitespace, no boxes or cards.
4. **Restraint over reflex.** One stellar-blue spot colour, motion that fits what it reveals, nothing decorative for its own sake. The minimal system dies by a thousand one-off exceptions.
5. **Write like he talks.** Copy is conversational and dry, never LLM-tidy. No em-dashes anywhere, including fiction. Concrete over generic, voice over polish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Accessibility is a requirement, not a pass. Semantic HTML, visible focus states, AA contrast minimum (body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1). Every animation ships a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative (crossfade or instant). Light, dark, and cream themes are all maintained at AA. Reveal animations enhance already-visible content; they never gate it.
